Restarting and resuming jobs¶

A failed job is not lost. It stays in the workspace with all its files, and an operator can fix what went wrong and revive it with a request. This page shows where a failed job is, what you may edit, which request to use, and how to resume a parent whose child failed. The request mechanics are in Task-manager usage in detail; the protocol is in Workflow filesystem API in detail.

Where a failed job is and how to read it¶

A failed job stays in jobs/failed/<placement>/...; workspace gc does not remove it. Three commands tell you what happened:

httk job show silicon        # prints the job directory
httk job why silicon         # explains the failure
httk job log silicon         # prints the run log

For a parent that failed at its wait, job show also lists its kept join and children.

The job directory holds:

Path

Content

job.json

The immutable declaration: workflow, parameters, placement, retry policy.

state.json

The owner-written state: current step, failure, counters, join.

run/

The persistent workdir, a.workdir in the runner.

attempts/<A>/

The control area of attempt A, including error.json.

logs/

The run log.

data/

The results the job committed.

.httk-job/

The runner’s own state, a.state, and the seal.

What you may edit¶

Everything in the job directory may be edited while the job is failed or paused, except job.json. It is digest-pinned at the first activation, and a different digest fails the job with protocol_error. That includes the retry policy, the parameters and the placement.

Edit before posting the request: a manager may launch the job the moment it is revived.

A revived step reruns from its top. Whatever the step itself regenerates, such as copying a template or writing inputs from parameters, it regenerates again and overwrites a hand edit made at that layer. Edit at the layer the step reads: a payload file under files/ that a step copies from, rather than the copy in run/; a file in run/ (an INCAR, say) only when the step uses it as it is. The runner’s documentation says which files a step regenerates.

The retry budgets in job.json (maximum_attempts_per_activation, maximum_total_attempts, maximum_activations; absent means unbounded) bind manual revival too. A revival past a budget fails right back with budget_exhausted or retry_exhausted. job.json keeps the declared budgets; to raise or lift one, post a set_budget request (see Raising a budget).

The three verbs¶

httk job request continue --reason "fixed the input" silicon
httk job request restart --reason "redo this step" silicon
httk job request override_step --step relax --reason "redo relax" silicon

Each applies to a failed or paused job. The CLI applies it at once to a job no manager holds and prints applied, queued, refused or uncertain; a job its owner holds applies it at the next boundary.

Action

Effect

continue

A job that failed at its wait on children (see below) returns to waiting with its retained join: no new attempt, no attempt budget counted. A wait that failed with budget_exhausted needs maximum_activations raised first. Any other failed job, and a paused job, returns to ready and the current step reruns as a new attempt, counted against the budget.

restart

Always ready: the current step reruns as a new attempt, counted against the budget. The failure is cleared and a retained join is dropped.

override_step --step STEP

ready with a new activation of STEP. STEP is checked against the steps the runner recorded unless --force. A retained join is dropped.

For a job that did not fail at a wait, continue and restart are the same. The attempt reason is manual_continue for both. Every rerun is bound by the effective retry budgets (the declared ones, with any set_budget override); only reopening a wait is not. job why says which applies.

--force applies to all three. It accepts the revival hazard described next, and publishes an override_step outside the recorded steps. Cancelled jobs cannot be revived by any request.

Raising a budget¶

A rerun past a retry budget is refused. With maximum_attempts_per_activation = 1, the first restart of a failed step fails right back with retry_exhausted. job.json cannot be edited, so the operator raises the limit with a request instead:

httk job why silicon
httk job request set_budget --max-attempts-per-activation 3 --reason "flaky node" silicon
httk job request restart --reason "retry with fixed INCAR" silicon

  1. job why names the exhausted budget and prints a hint with the set_budget command, naming the option for that budget.

  2. Fix the input, as described above.

  3. set_budget raises the limit. The job stays where it is, in the state and at the priority it had, and no attempt starts.

  4. restart (or continue) revives the job. It is now within the budget.

set_budget takes at least one of --max-attempts-per-activation N, --max-total-attempts N and --max-activations N. Each is an integer of 1 or more, or unlimited to lift that limit. The options are valid only with set_budget. The request applies to any job that is not succeeded or cancelled, including a ready or running one; on a running attempt it applies at the next boundary, like every request. It does not need --force.

The values go into state.json as budget_override. Later requests merge into it: a key set again takes the later value, and a key not named keeps its earlier value. A limit with no override falls back to job.json. The effective policy is the declared limits with the override applied. The manager’s launch check, automatic retries, the maximum_activations checks and the revival checks of continue, restart and override_step all use it. job.json and the provenance record of the job keep the declared policy.

job why and job show print the budget counters against the effective limit, and the declared one where it differs:

attempts this activation  1/3 (declared 1)

The JSON output carries the declared values and budget_override in its budgets mapping. Each applied set_budget also records the limits it set in the job’s state.json history (the request_applied entry carries budget).

A parent that failed at its wait keeps its join, and continue reopens it with no new attempt. Reopening a dependency_failure wait needs no budget. A parent that failed with budget_exhausted (its join would advance past maximum_activations) needs that limit raised first, then continue; the join re-decides under the raised budget and advances to its next step, with no new children:

httk job request set_budget --max-activations 5 --reason "one more step" PARENT
httk job request continue --reason "budget raised" PARENT

restart spawns new children and counts an attempt, so it needs the attempt budget (see Restarting the parent instead).

A limit can also be lowered. A limit set below what the job has already used ends the job at its next budget check: a ready job fails with budget_exhausted at its next claim. That is a way to stop further automatic retries.

Do not try the other routes. Editing job.json fails the job at the digest check. Counters are not reset, since that would lose the record of what the job did. --force does not skip a budget: the revival request checks the budget whether or not --force is given (--force only accepts the revival hazard), and the manager checks it again at launch. override_step --step STEP is the alternative to set_budget: it starts a new activation, bound by maximum_activations.

Parents and children¶

A parent that waits on children with gather() fails when its join can no longer be satisfied, because a child failed or was cancelled. The parent records what it saw of each child (observations) and, when the gather named no on_impossible step, fails with dependency_failure at its wait. It keeps its join in state.json, and that retained join is what lets you reopen the wait. The children stay where they failed. A child that runs while its parent is failed still finds it through a.parent, since the parent stays at its placement. A parent can also fail at its wait with budget_exhausted: its join is satisfied (or rescued), but the advance would pass maximum_activations. It keeps its join then too; see Raising a budget.

Fix the child, then continue the parent¶

httk job why PARENT
httk job request continue --reason "fixed the INCAR" CHILD
httk job show CHILD
httk job log CHILD
httk job request continue --reason "child succeeded" PARENT

  1. job why PARENT says the job failed while waiting on its children, lists each child that did not succeed with its failure, and notes that continue reopens the wait while restart reruns the step and spawns new children.

  2. Fix the child as described above, then continue it. Reviving a child that the parent’s recorded observations name needs --force, unless that parent’s join still names the child: the parent failed at its wait, or waits on it again. This flow needs no --force. Continuing the parent first is accepted too, but the manager re-decides the reopened wait within one pass and, while the child is still failed, fails the parent again at once. That is harmless: continue it again after the child succeeded.

  3. Watch the child with job show and job log until it succeeds.

  4. continue the parent. It returns to waiting with the same join, with no new attempt. On its next pass the manager re-evaluates the join from the children’s current states. If it is satisfied, the gathering step runs with the children as a.children, as usual. If a child is still failed or cancelled, the parent fails again with dependency_failure at once.

Without --tree, continue on such a parent prints a note on stderr for each join child that is still failed or cancelled, or not found at its placement, so you know the reopened wait will fail again.

Nested trees¶

Failures propagate upward: when a child’s own wait fails, that child fails with dependency_failure, and its parent’s join becomes impossible in turn. Every level keeps its join. To resume the whole chain at once:

httk job request continue --tree --reason "inputs fixed" PARENT

--tree also resumes the failed descendants reachable through retained joins, deepest first, before the parent. Each is an ordinary continue request, posted and served one job at a time in that order. If a descendant’s request is not applied (queued behind its owner, refused, or its revival fails at a budget), the CLI stops, posts nothing for the remaining jobs, prints not posted: <job_key> ... for each and exits 1; resolve that job and rerun continue --tree. Children in other states (succeeded, paused, running, cancelled) are left alone. Join children --tree leaves alone (not found, cancelled, or held by an owner at that moment) are reported with a note, as without --tree. Fix the leaf failures first: a descendant that fails for its own reason, not at a wait, returns to ready and reruns its step, which may fail again. --tree is valid only with continue.

Restarting the parent instead¶

restart on a parent that failed at its wait reruns the spawning step, which creates new children. The old children stay where they are. Terminal ones can be removed with httk job delete. The spawning step’s attempt counts against the budget: with maximum_attempts_per_activation = 1, restart fails right back with retry_exhausted. Raise the limit with httk job request set_budget --max-attempts-per-activation N first, then restart. Alternatively, httk job request override_step --step <spawning step> reruns that step as a new activation, bound by maximum_activations. The restarted parent no longer waits on them, so reviving one of them needs --force, and the parent will not notice it.

Cancelled and deleted children¶

A cancelled child cannot be revived by any request, so a join naming it stays impossible: continue on the parent fails again at once. restart the parent or override_step it instead. A deleted child makes a reopened wait fail with dependency_failure after the join grace, a manager option that defaults to one hour.

Parents with on_impossible¶

A parent whose gather named an on_impossible step advanced to that step instead of failing, which consumed the observation and cleared the join (unless that advance failed with budget_exhausted, which keeps it). Reviving its child then still needs --force, and the parent does not notice the revived child. The wait cannot be reopened. To have the parent use the revived child, rerun a step that gathers it again (a runner step that calls gather(..., rejoin=[label]), via override_step), or restart or override_step the spawning step to spawn new children.

Paused jobs¶

A job pauses on a pause request or when the runner calls a.pause(reason). It stays where it is until you continue it, which reruns the current step as a new attempt, or restart it, which does the same; edit its files first, as for a failed job. override_step moves it to a named step.

httk job request continue --reason "quota restored" silicon

Remote workspaces¶

continue --tree works on local workspaces only; it is refused for a remote --workspace binding. For a remote workspace, post one continue per failed job yourself, deepest first, then the parent. Remotes covers what a remote binding accepts.
